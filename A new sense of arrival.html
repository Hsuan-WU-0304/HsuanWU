A New Sense Arrival of Shih-Lin

Architecture as an Urban Banner

How can architecture translate urban observation into a distinct identity for Shilin?

New Sense of Arrival begins with an observation of Taipei’s MRT Red Line, examining how stations are recognized through distinctive urban markers, architectural forms, and surrounding landscapes. Together, these elements establish recognizable identities along the journey, signaling the experience of arriving at different parts of the city. Translating this observation to Shilin, the project explores how architecture can establish a distinct identity for the district. Four approaches to the site construct different perceptions of arrival, connecting Shilin Station with the existing Shih-Lin Paper Mill and its surrounding urban fabric.The proposal introduces a new cultural layer to the historic site: a modern art museum and renovated heritage buildings form the public ground, while an elevated art research center becomes an urban banner for Shilin—a recognizable architectural presence in dialogue with the Taipei Performing Arts Center and Shilin’s existing skyline.

Reading Arrival : MRT Red Line / Signage, Thresholds and Urban Markers

Tracing the MRT Red Line from Taipei Main Station toward Shilin, the study examines how the experience of arrival shifts from underground graphic signage to above-ground architectural markers. Ten distinctive structures around Yuanshan and Jiantan were documented as visual anchors along the journey, raising a question for Shilin: what could become its architectural anchor, and how might architecture create a distinct sense of arrival?

What urban structure surrounds the site?

The field mapping expands the observation of arrival from individual landmarks to the larger urban structure of Shilin. By tracing architectural anchors, urban fabric, movement networks, and visual connections, the site emerges as a point of convergence between the MRT corridor, surrounding districts, cultural institutions, river landscape, and mountains. These relationships establish the site not as an isolated object, but as a potential new urban reference within Shilin.

Four Senses of Arrival : Translating Urban Approaches into Architectural Markers

Four primary approaches to the site generate four distinct experiences of arrival. From the MRT, the elevated tower acts as a distant urban marker; from the western urban fabric, the commercial volume forms a gateway into the site; through the historic paper mill, the museum establishes a pedestrian-scale entrance; and from Shilin Night Market, the elevated art research center appears as a horizontal urban banner. Together, these conditions transform arrival from a single entrance into a sequence of architectural identities perceived across the city.
