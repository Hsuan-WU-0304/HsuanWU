Eduhabitat

Adaptive Housing System for School Transformation

How can vacant school spaces become a new infrastructure for urban housing?

EduHabitat responds to two emerging urban conditions in Taiwan: declining school enrollment and increasing housing demand. Rather than treating underused school buildings as obsolete structures, the project explores their potential to accommodate new forms of collective living while retaining their civic role within the neighborhood. The proposal transforms an existing elementary school in Wanhua through a modular infill housing system. A lightweight structural framework is inserted into the existing school fabric, allowing adaptable micro-housing units to accommodate different household types while preserving the original structural logic. Beyond housing provision, the project considers school-to-housing conversion as a strategy for urban regeneration. Shared spaces, existing school grounds, and connections to the surrounding historic fabric extend domestic life into a larger communal network, allowing the former school to remain a piece of neighborhood infrastructure.

A Kit of Parts for Adaptable Living

Rather than designing each housing type as a fixed unit, EduHabitat establishes a shared kit of parts that can be recombined across different household configurations. Structural frames, service pods, storage, kitchens, partitions, and façade components form a common construction system, allowing working suites and one-, two-, and three-bedroom units to emerge from the same modular logic. Standardization therefore becomes a framework for adaptability rather than uniformity.

A Parametric Infill Strategy for School Transformation

Working within the existing structural grid of the underused school, a parametric generator automatically arranges predefined 1BDR, 2BDR, and 3BDR units together with shared spaces. The algorithm evaluates possible configurations and generates an optimized layout, increasing allocation efficiency while adapting the housing system to the constraints of the existing structure.
