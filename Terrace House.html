Terrace House

Dormitory Housing as an Inhabited Landscape

How can dormitory housing become an inhabited landscape?

Jingxin Lake Community reinterprets the garden-based living of the existing lakeside villas as collective housing for engineers. Private gardens are translated vertically into balconies and terraces, allowing each dwelling to maintain a direct relationship with outdoor space while forming a new inhabited landscape along the lake.

YEAR
2024
TYPE
Academic Work
LOCATION
Hsinchu, Taiwan
PROGRAM
Dormitory Housing
INSTRUCTOR
Chien-Shuo Pai (Architecture via Time)

From Block to Landscape

Through horizontal expansion and vertical stacking, the residential block is transformed into a stepped landscape. A 600 × 600 mm modular grid organizes the dwelling system, allowing one-, two-, and three-bedroom units to expand through different combinations. Terraces and private gardens emerge through the shifting volumes, providing daylight, ventilation, and direct access to outdoor space while forming a stepped skyline in dialogue with the surrounding landscape and lakeside views.

Dwelling as a Collective Landscape

The garden-based living of the existing lakeside villas is translated into a collective housing system, where balconies, terraces, and shared outdoor spaces extend domestic life vertically through the building.
